01 · GTM Strategy

Win the organizer, not the whole group

Segment

  • Extended family trips: multiple households, grandparents to cousins
  • Each household pays its own share, unevenly
  • Recurs around reunions and holidays

Motion & goal

  • Hybrid: PLG primary (invite inside a trip, fast time-to-value)
  • CLG secondary: families recommend it to other family groups
  • Goal: Retain existing Roamly travelers hitting a wall

Channel & metric

  • Existing users with booking history and prior group drop-off
  • Prompt directly: "planning a trip with others? try Groups"
  • Metric: organizer repeat-organize rate, not full-group adoption
For Roamly's existing travelers who already coordinate group trips manually, Roamly Groups reduces the organizer's burden immediately, even if not everyone fully adopts it.

Key message 1

Less coordination, not perfect coordination. Any adoption beats today's manual work.

Key message 2

You stay in control without doing it all manually: sync and split in one place, with fallback for holdouts.

Key message 3

Built for the trip you're already planning.

02 · Competitive Intel & Strategic Bet

Coordination is free. Booking is the moat.

Battlecard · TripProf

  • Offer: all-in-one trip planner: itinerary, multi-currency splitting, checklists, documents, offline. No booking layer.
  • Positioning: "A whole trip, not just the bill"; family-trip and reunion playbook
  • Pricing: freemium + credits (€3.99/credit, Pro €7.99/mo); collaboration is paywalled
  • Why the biggest threat: already owns the multi-household family framing and has a live monetization engine

The insight

  • TripProf wins by monetizing content, not transactions
  • So coordination was never Roamly's moat
  • Roamly's edge: free default coordination paired with a marketplace of vetted, bookable local experiences
  • Biggest threat: if booking doesn't happen in the same flow, Groups is a free feature with nothing to show for it

The bet

Roamly Groups wins by keeping the shared itinerary and the experience marketplace connected, not separate, so booking happens inside the plan the family is already coordinating in.

Deprioritizing

Packing checklists, document storage, offline mode, general destination content. The itinerary stays: it is what connects coordination to a booking.

05 · Pricing Recommendation

Planning is free. Booking carries a small fee.

Model & goal

  • Transaction-based: small fee on the booking, shown as its own line at checkout
  • Goal: more family groups planning and booking on Roamly; easy to try, easy to recommend
  • Families travel once or twice a year, so no monthly charge

Who pays

  • Each household pays its share of the booking and of the fee, via split payments
  • The organizer feels the value but doesn't carry the cost

Packaging · by features

  • Everything a normal trip needs is in the default package
  • Booking is never blocked
  • One optional extra for harder trips

Groups · default

Shared plan, invites, split payments, synced dates, booking. Fee inside the booking, split across households. Route most families here.

Groups Plus · optional

For big or tricky trips, e.g. several generations with different needs. Extra planning help plus flexible booking if a household drops out. Paid on top, per trip.

Validate · in this order

  • Max-Diff: which features matter and are worth paying extra for (Plus ideas vs. default)
  • Van Westendorp: acceptable range for the booking fee and the Plus price, by household size and generations

The one thing that changes everything

That families who plan on Roamly will book on Roamly. Both revenue lines depend on it. If they plan here and book elsewhere, we earn nothing.

06 · Individual Insights

What this process taught me

Friction points

  • Positioning was the hardest to finalize. I kept writing features instead of what the family actually gets. More examples would have helped.
  • Messaging has to change depending on the audience. It took me a while to see that.

Aha moment

Positioning and messaging are two very different jobs. It's important to split them, but you need both.

Key takeaways

  • Have a success metric that proves the bet. It makes everything more concrete, and you can tell if things are working or not.
  • Follow the whole process. It helped a lot. After finishing one step, the next one is so much easier.
