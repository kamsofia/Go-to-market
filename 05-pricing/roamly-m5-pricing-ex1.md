# Roamly Groups · Pricing & Packaging Recommendation, Module 5 (Exercise 1)

## Responses
- **Pricing goal (Where to win first):** Get more family groups planning and booking on Roamly. Start with the travelers already on Roamly, then spread to the families they bring in. The price should be easy to try and easy to recommend.
- **Why (Justification):** Roamly's existing travelers are the fastest to win, because they already book with us and get stuck the moment family joins. But the trip is also where new families find out Roamly can do this. An organizer will tell the next family about it, and the price is part of what they say. A competitor charges for group planning and can't book anything, so a price that's easy to explain is a way to stand out. The price should be small and clear enough that organizers happily recommend it, and that they don't slip back to WhatsApp and a spreadsheet.
- **Pricing model (The model):** Transaction-based. A small fee is added to the booking a family makes through Roamly Groups. Planning together costs nothing extra, and the fee shows as its own line at checkout. Families travel once or twice a year, so we charge on the trip they book, not every month.
- **Who pays (Buyer vs. user):** The households pay, not the organizer alone. Each household pays its share of the booking and its share of the fee, using the split payments Groups already has. The organizer is the one who feels the value (less coordination work), but they don't carry the cost.
- **Packaging (The structure):** By Features. What they need to see is that planning is free and a small fee applies only when the family books. Everything a normal trip needs is in the default package, and booking is never blocked. The only difference is an optional extra for trips that are harder to organize, so I'm packaging by features.
- **Tiers (What each is for):** Groups (default): shared plan, invites, split payments, synced dates, and booking. Small fee inside the booking, split across households. Route most families here.

Groups Plus (optional): for organizers with a big or tricky trip, like several generations with different needs. Extra help planning it, plus flexible booking if a household drops out. Paid on top, per trip.
- **Which research method (Van Westendorp, Max-Diff, Conjoint) would you commission, and what specific assumption are you trying to validate?:** Two steps, in this order:

1. Max-Diff first. Ask Roamly travelers who have organized a group trip to pick the most and least valuable option from short sets of features. The list would include the ideas for Plus (extra help planning a tricky trip, flexible booking if a household drops out) next to what's already in the default package. The result shows which features matter, and which are worth paying extra for.

2. Van Westendorp second. Ask the same people about price, twice: the fee added to a booking, and the price of Plus per trip, using the features Max-Diff ranked highest. Four questions each: a bargain, fair, getting expensive, too expensive. Record household size and generations, so we can compare by type of family.

What we're validating: which features belong in Plus, and that both the fee and the Plus price sit in a range families accept.
- **What is the one thing in your recommendation that would change everything if you got it wrong?:** That families who plan on Roamly will book on Roamly. Both ways we earn depend on it. The booking fee only exists if the booking happens here, and Plus is an add-on to that same trip. Planning is free, so if families plan with us and book elsewhere, we earn nothing and have given away what TripProf sells. Whether the fee and Plus are big enough is a second question, and it only matters once bookings happen.
