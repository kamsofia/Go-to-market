# Roamly Groups · Messaging Pillars & AI-Generated Asset, Module 4 (Exercise 1)

## Responses
- **FROM (Life before):** An extended family already booking trips on Roamly individually hits a wall the moment they try to do it together. Each household has to open its own separate booking, chase relatives for who's paying what by hand, and manually check that everyone's actually free on the same dates. The product they already trust for solo trips gets in their way the moment more than one household is involved — so the plan ends up living somewhere else, in a spreadsheet or a group chat, even though nobody wanted to leave Roamly in the first place.
- **TO (Life after):** That same family plans and books the trip together, as one group, inside Roamly — turning the plan into real booked experiences, without ever having to leave the product to hold it together.
- **Total number of audiences that need their own message:** One: the organizer (which covers anyone doing that coordinating role, whether it's one person or split across a couple of them — same pain, same message)
- **Justification, why does each one need its own pillar?:** Only one distinct pillar is needed because every candidate audience we tested collapses into the same person with the same pain:

Participating family members — don't have an activation surface (no channel or campaign targets them directly; they only encounter Groups via the organizer's invite), and Roamly's finalized channel approach explicitly makes the organizer, not the group, the activation point.
The "Advocate" (CLG motion) — describes how Groups spreads through word of mouth, not a person Roamly writes messaging to. There's no touchpoint to deploy a message at "someone who might mention this at a reunion."
Co-planners / multiple people helping organize — same job, same pain as the primary organizer (chasing relatives, aligning dates/payments across households without one person doing it all manually). Nothing in the segmentation criteria (role, use case, sophistication, journey stage, geography, price sensitivity) separates them from the organizer — they'd be moved by the identical message.
- **Audience 1:** The Organizer
- **🎯 Value Prop (The promise):** Less coordination work, from day one — plan and book the trip together, as one group, without chasing every household separately.
- **🏆 Capabilities (The proof points):** Shared itinerary planning across households; in-app split payments so each household pays its own share; synced availability so dates get confirmed without back-and-forth; booking real experiences directly from the shared plan.
- **📊 Evidence (The receipts):** 82% of organizers who tried Groups organized a second family trip within 4 months.
