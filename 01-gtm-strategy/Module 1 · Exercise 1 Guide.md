# Roamly Groups · GTM Strategy, Module 1 (Exercise 1)

## Responses
- **What is the core business problem Roamly is trying to solve with Roamly Groups?:** Roamly doesn't support bookings when they involve more than one person - driving high drop-off rates and pushing users into manual workarounds outside the platform.
- **What does success look like if this launch goes well? Think beyond revenue, the product, the brand, and the customer relationship.:** Group repeat rates increase, host earnings increase, and Roamly differentiates from competitors that don't support group bookings.
- **What signals suggest this is a real market opportunity worth pursuing? What is the behavioral data telling you about unmet demand?:** Users are already trying to coordinate group bookings manually through separate accounts, they are flagging issues to the support team.
- **List your top 5 possible segments that could benefit from Roamly Groups.:** Extended family trips, friend trips, company offsites, school trips, and wedding trips.
- **How might you size the market? Think industry reports, travel research, social listening, competitor analysis.:** Internal data: find in which segment current users belong, check travel industry reports for group travel growth and trends, and scan competitors.
- **Which cross-functional stakeholders would you include to validate and execute? Think beyond product and engineering, who has context on the customer, market, and commercial model?:** Customer-facing teams (cs, sales, support), marketing, analytics,
- **Which one segment is the most valuable to target first, and why?:** Extended family trips — multiple households (siblings, cousins, grandparents), not just parents and kids. Each household pays its own share unevenly, and coordinating dates and activities across generations (kids, mobility, dietary needs, different interests) is genuinely hard to align manually. This is exactly what split payments and shared itinerary planning solve, and it recurs naturally around reunions and holidays.
- **What is the core value proposition for that segment?:** One shared plan for the whole family — each household pays their own share, aligns on dates, and picks activities everyone actually wants, instead of coordinating manually across separate accounts and chat threads.
