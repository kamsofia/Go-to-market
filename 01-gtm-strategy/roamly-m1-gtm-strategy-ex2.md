- **Which motion did you choose, PLG, SLG, CLG, or a hybrid? Explain why; if hybrid, what are your primary vs. secondary layers?:** Hybrid: PLG primary, CLG secondary. Within a trip, buyer and user are the same people, time to value is fast, and it's self-serve — one family member invites the rest of the household group in via the product itself. Across trips, growth also benefits from peer influence: extended families naturally recommend what worked to other family groups at gatherings and reunions, which is advocacy-driven, not product-mechanical. Primary layer: PLG for adoption within a trip. Secondary layer: CLG for spreading to new family groups.


# V1 GTM Strategy One-Pager — Roamly Groups

## Positioning statement:
For Roamly's existing travelers who already coordinate group trips manually, Roamly Groups reduces the organizer's burden immediately — even if not everyone in the group fully adopts it — replacing today's separate accounts, spreadsheets, and manual payment splitting with one shared plan.

## Key messages:

1. Less coordination, not perfect coordination: Roamly Groups works even when not everyone in your group is fully on board — any adoption is less manual work than today.
2. You stay in control, without doing it all manually: Syncing availability and splitting payments happens in one place, with fallback for holdouts.
3. Built for the trip you're already planning: Designed around the coordination Roamly's own users are already attempting today.

## Recommended channel approach:
Target existing Roamly users with a booking history and any prior group-session drop-off, since they're already organizers in practice. Prompt them directly ("planning a trip with others? try Groups") rather than targeting the wider group — the organizer, not the group, is the activation point.

## Suggested success metric:
Organizer repeat-organize rate — the share of organizers who use Roamly Groups again for a subsequent trip — rather than full-group adoption rate, since the goal is reducing organizer burden and protecting their loyalty, not requiring every household to convert.
