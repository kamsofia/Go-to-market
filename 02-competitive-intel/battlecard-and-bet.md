# Roamly Groups · Competitive Battlecard & Strategic Bet, Module 2 (Exercise 2)

## Responses
- **Who is Roamly Groups actually for, based on everything you now know?:** Extended family trips — multiple households — where each household has its own budget and payment method, but shares the pain of one person fronting costs and manually chasing everyone's preferences.
- **What is the one thing that makes it worth choosing over the competitor?:** Roamly pairs free, default coordination with a real marketplace of vetted, bookable local experiences — TripProf only gives you the plan, never the thing you're planning for.
- **What is the biggest threat to that position, based on your battlecard?:** Coordination alone has no defensible moat — TripProf proves it can be given away free. If Groups only solves logistics and the booking never happens in the same flow, you've built a free feature with nothing to show for it.
- **Of all the signals you’re seeing, which one changes how you think about the market? Hint: looking at how your primary competitor wins, what does that tell you about what Roamly Groups needs to do differently to take that buyer?:** TripProf wins by monetizing content (destination insights), not transactions — which tells you the coordination layer itself was never Roamly's moat. What actually needs to win the buyer isn't better planning tools; it's making the booking step feel like the natural conclusion of planning, not a separate decision the family can walk away from.
- **Complete the sentence: “I now believe that Roamly Groups wins when because”:** I now believe that Roamly Groups wins when the shared itinerary surfaces bookable experiences inline, so booking is the path of least resistance rather than an extra step — because coordination alone is replicable for free, but coordination-that-converts-into-a-booking is not something a planning-only tool like TripProf can copy without becoming a marketplace itself.
- **“I am betting that Roamly Groups wins by”, your strategic commitment, not what you’re building but how you’ll win with it.:** I am betting that Roamly Groups wins by keeping the shared itinerary and the experience marketplace connected, not separate — so whenever a family is ready to book, it happens inside the same plan they're already coordinating in, instead of them having to leave Roamly (or start over somewhere else) to make it happen.
- **“To make this bet, I am deprioritizing”, every strategic bet means giving something up. Naming the sacrifice is the proof a real decision was made.:** To make this bet, I am deprioritizing the parts of trip planning that have nothing to do with booking — packing checklists, document/passport storage, offline mode, general destination content. The itinerary itself stays in scope, since it's what connects coordination to a booking. Groups isn't trying to match TripProf's planning breadth, just the slice of planning that leads somewhere bookable.
